\begin{abstract}
This report is Part~2 of the PneumoFusionNet term project.
In Part~1 we found that an image-only DenseNet-121 trained on MIMIC-CXR
chest X-rays reached AUC\,$\approx$\,0.808.
Part~2 adds two further sources of information and evaluates four models
on the same held-out test set (562 radiographs from 511 patients).
In \textbf{Phase~2} we feed the INDICATION and FINDINGS sections of the
radiology report into Bio\_ClinicalBERT~\cite{alsentzer2019bert} and join
it to the image via cross-attention, raising AUC to \textbf{0.9475} and
Sensitivity to \textbf{87.1\%}.
In \textbf{Phase~3} we additionally fuse a WBC count
(the only blood-test result available within 15~min of admission) alongside
16 structured clinical features, reaching AUC\,=\,\textbf{0.9478}.
A four-way ablation (CXR~$|$~CXR+Text~$|$~CXR+WBC~$|$~CXR+Text+WBC)
shows that the radiology report accounts for almost all the AUC gain (+13.9 pp),
while WBC provides a marginal further lift (+0.1 pp).\\
\textbf{Index Terms---} Chest X-Ray, Radiology Report, WBC, MIMIC-CXR,
Pneumonia Detection, Multimodal Fusion.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Pneumonia Detection, CXR, Radiology Text, WBC, Multimodal Fusion, MIMIC-CXR

\section{Introduction}
% ==========================================================

Pneumonia kills around 2.5 million people every year~\cite{who2023}, and the
chest X-ray (CXR) is still the first diagnostic step in most hospitals.
Even experienced radiologists sometimes disagree on whether a faint shadow
represents pneumonia, fluid, or atelectasis.
Our Part~1 experiments showed that a DenseNet-121 image classifier tops out
at AUC\,=\,0.808, because the image alone cannot encode the patient's
inflammatory state or clinical history.

Two information sources that \emph{are} routinely available in a real
emergency department fill this gap.
First, the \textbf{radiology report}: the attending radiologist writes a brief
paragraph containing an INDICATION (why the patient came in) and FINDINGS
(what the scan shows).
We use only these two sections and explicitly remove the IMPRESSION, which
states the diagnosis — allowing the model to read it would constitute
label leakage.
Second, the \textbf{WBC count}: part of every routine Complete Blood Count
(CBC) and returned within 15~minutes of admission.
An elevated WBC is an early systemic marker of infection.

Part~2 builds four models on a \emph{shared, frozen patient-level split}
and evaluates every model on the \emph{identical} 562-image test partition
so that all numbers are directly comparable:
\[
  \underbrace{\text{CXR}}_{\text{Phase 1}}
  \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{CXR + Text}}_{\text{Phase 2}}
  \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{CXR + WBC}}_{\text{Exp 5C}}
  \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{CXR + Text + WBC}}_{\text{Phase 3}}
\]
All experiments use the restricted MIMIC-CXR
dataset~\cite{johnson2019mimic} (PhysioNet DUA + CITI ethics training
required for access).

% ==========================================================
\section{Dataset and Experimental Setup}
% ==========================================================

\subsection{Data Sources}

We used \textbf{MIMIC-CXR-JPG~v2.0.0}~\cite{johnson2019mimic}, a large
chest X-ray dataset from Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Centre (2011--2016).
Only \textbf{PA (postero-anterior) views} were retained, as mixing lateral
or AP-portable views degraded performance in earlier pilots.
WBC counts were sourced from \textbf{MIMIC-IV}~\cite{johnson2023mimiciv}
lab records.

\subsection{Experiment 1 — Patient-Level Split}

The final paired dataset contains \textbf{3,763 radiographs from 3,404
unique patients} (1,872 Normal, 1,891 Pneumonia, nearly balanced).
The split was performed \emph{once} at the patient level using
\texttt{subject\_id}, so the same person cannot appear in more than one
partition:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrl}
\toprule
Partition & Patients & Radiographs & Class balance \\
\midrule
Train & 2,382 (70.0\%) & 2,635 & 1,311 N / 1,324 P \\
Val   &   511 (15.0\%) &   566 &   278 N / 288 P \\
Test  &   511 (15.0\%) &   562 &   283 N / 279 P \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent Zero patient overlap was verified across all three splits
(Train$\cap$Val\,=\,Train$\cap$Test\,=\,Val$\cap$Test\,=\,$\emptyset$).
Every model was trained on the same 2,635 images and evaluated on the
same 562 test images.

\subsection{Experiment 2 — Image Backbone}

The initial design used \texttt{densenet121-res224-all} from
TorchXRayVision~\cite{cohen2022torchxrayvision}, whose pretraining mixture
\emph{includes} MIMIC-CXR.
To eliminate any overlap between the backbone's pretraining data and our
test set, we switched to \textbf{\texttt{densenet121-res224-nih}}, pretrained
exclusively on NIH ChestX-ray14 — with \textbf{zero MIMIC pretraining
overlap}.

\subsection{WBC Matching and Missingness}

WBC values were matched using patient and admission identifiers within a
$\pm$2-day window around the index radiograph.
3,029 of 3,763 (80.5\%) records received a matched WBC value;
the remaining 734 (19.5\%) were imputed with the \emph{training-set median}
($7.40\times10^{9}$\,/L).
The imputed value was computed \emph{only} from the training partition and
applied identically to validation and test sets (no leakage).
Because the matching window is retrospective, the WBC analysis is framed
as an \textbf{exploratory analysis} rather than a real-time ED biomarker.

\subsection{Report Preprocessing (Experiment 4)}

Radiology reports were parsed to extract the \textbf{INDICATION} and
\textbf{FINDINGS} sections only.
The IMPRESSION section (which states the diagnosis) was explicitly excluded.
The raw-report fallback (previously used when section extraction failed)
was removed: 218 out of 3,763 reports (5.79\%) returned an empty string
after extraction; these were passed as empty tokens and documented.
The BERT attention mask is forwarded to the cross-attention layer as
\texttt{key\_padding\_mask} so that padding tokens do not influence the
attended representation.

% ==========================================================
\section{Model Architectures}
% ==========================================================

\subsection{Phase~1 — CXR Only (Baseline)}

A DenseNet-121~\cite{huang2017densenet} pretrained on NIH ChestX-ray14
(TorchXRayVision~\cite{cohen2022torchxrayvision}) acts as the image encoder.
A CBAM~\cite{woo2018cbam} channel-and-spatial attention block is attached
after the penultimate feature map.
The encoder outputs a \textbf{1024-d} embedding, which feeds a small
classifier (Dropout\,$\to$\,Linear\,$\to$\,BN\,$\to$\,ReLU\,$\times$2\,$\to$\,Linear).
Training used Focal Loss~\cite{lin2017focal} ($\gamma=2.0$) and a single
70/15/15 patient split.

\subsection{Phase~2 — CXR + Radiology Text}

The cleaned report (INDICATION\,+\,FINDINGS) is tokenised and fed to
\textbf{Bio\_ClinicalBERT}~\cite{alsentzer2019bert}; the last two Transformer
layers are fine-tuned (lr\,=\,1e-5).
An 8-head cross-attention layer lets the image query the text:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{c} = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{img}}
  \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{txt}}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{txt}},
  \quad d_k = 64
  \label{eq:crossattn}
\end{equation}
The BERT attention mask is passed as \texttt{key\_padding\_mask}.
Fused representation: \textbf{1024\,+\,512\,=\,1536-d}.
Training: Focal Loss ($\gamma=2.0$), Mixup~\cite{zhang2018mixup} ($\alpha=0.2$),
image-text head lr\,=\,2e-4.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Threshold Selection}

For every model, the classification threshold $\tau$ was chosen by
Youden's~J index on the \emph{validation set only}
($\tau^* = \arg\max_\tau[\mathrm{Sensitivity}+\mathrm{Specificity}-1]$),
then frozen before evaluating on the test set.
No test-set information was used at any stage of threshold selection.

\subsection{Phase~1 Detail (Experiment 3)}

The image-only baseline (Model~A, $\tau_{\mathrm{val}}=0.571$) achieves
ROC-AUC\,=\,0.808, PR-AUC\,=\,0.820, Accuracy\,=\,72.4\%,
Sensitivity\,=\,61.7\%, Specificity\,=\,83.0\%, F1\,=\,0.689.
Confusion matrix: TN\,=\,235, FP\,=\,48, FN\,=\,107, TP\,=\,172.

\subsection{Phase~2 Detail — Text Preprocessing (Experiment 4)}

Sections used: INDICATION + FINDINGS only (IMPRESSION excluded).
Raw-report fallback: \textbf{disabled}.
218 reports (5.79\%) were empty after extraction and treated as empty
token sequences.
A text-only baseline (ClinicalBERT classifier, no image) reached
AUC\,=\,0.940, confirming that report text and diagnosis labels are
strongly correlated in MIMIC-CXR and that the text modality carries
most of the predictive signal.

\section{Experiment 6 — Clinical Feature Cleanup}
% ==========================================================

The Phase~3 triple-fusion model uses 16 structured clinical features
(age, gender, vital signs, and lab values).
Based on supervisor feedback, \texttt{is\_deceased} was permanently removed
from the feature set because it encodes the outcome rather than a
pre-diagnosis observation.
Median imputation and StandardScaler normalisation were fitted
\emph{exclusively on the training partition} and applied identically to
validation and test sets.
The same 70/15/15 patient split was used throughout.
Final feature list (16): \textit{age, gender\_M, heart\_rate,
respiratory\_rate, SpO2, systolic\_bp, diastolic\_bp, temperature\_F,
WBC, haemoglobin, haematocrit, creatinine, CRP, alkaline\_phosphatase,
albumin, has\_vitals}.

\section{Conclusion}
% ==========================================================

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,leftmargin=*,topsep=3pt]

  \item \textbf{The X-ray alone is an insufficient classifier.}
    Even with CBAM attention and a clean NIH-pretrained backbone, the
    image-only model saturates at AUC\,=\,0.808, with Sensitivity of
    only 61.7\% — missing more than a third of pneumonia cases.

  \item \textbf{The radiology report accounts for almost all the gain.}
    Adding INDICATION\,+\,FINDINGS text (with IMPRESSION removed) raises
    AUC by \textbf{+13.9 pp} to 0.948 and Sensitivity to 87.1\%.
    A text-only baseline achieves AUC\,=\,0.940, confirming that the
    report is the dominant modality.

  \item \textbf{WBC provides only a marginal further lift.}
    Model~C (CXR\,+\,WBC) barely outperforms the image baseline
    (AUC\,=\,0.814), and Model~D (CXR\,+\,Text\,+\,WBC) adds only
    0.1 pp over Model~B.
    WBC matching was retrospective ($\pm$2-day window); real-time
    pre-index availability requires future prospective validation.

  \item \textbf{All results are on a shared, zero-leakage test set.}
    Patient-level splits (zero overlap), validation-only threshold
    selection, training-only imputation, and a MIMIC-free backbone
    ensure the numbers are not inflated by data leakage.

  \item \textbf{Next step — test on Indian patient data.}
    All experiments use a US hospital dataset (MIMIC-CXR).
    TB is common in India, malnutrition alters X-ray patterns,
    and scanner / reporting styles differ markedly from Western centres.
    A prospective study on Indian ED data will determine whether the
    CXR\,+\,Text model generalises or requires fine-tuning.

\end{enumerate}
