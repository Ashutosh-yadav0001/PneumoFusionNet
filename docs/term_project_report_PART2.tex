\begin{abstract}
This report presents \textbf{PneumoFusionNet Part~2}, a progressive multimodal
deep learning study for pneumonia detection on the restricted
\textbf{MIMIC-CXR}~\cite{johnson2019mimic} dataset.
Starting from the Part~1 conclusion that image-only DenseNet-121 plateaus at
AUC\,$\approx$\,0.826, Part~2 fuses three clinical modalities.
\textbf{Phase~2} adds leakage-free radiology text (FINDINGS\,+\,HISTORY)
via Bio\_ClinicalBERT~\cite{alsentzer2019bert} and 8-head cross-attention,
reaching \textbf{AUC\,=\,0.949}, Sensitivity\,=\,91.4\%.
\textbf{Phase~3c} --- the primary contribution of this report --- is a
\emph{minimal} triple fusion model using only a single biomarker:
CXR image\,+\,radiology text\,+\,\textbf{WBC count} from routine CBC.
This clinically practical combination achieves \textbf{AUC\,=\,0.9711},
\textbf{Sensitivity\,=\,92.25\%}, and \textbf{Specificity\,=\,93.59\%}
on a 3,763-image scale-up cohort---adding \textbf{+2.51\% AUC} over the
text-only model while requiring only one additional lab value.

\textbf{Index Terms---} Multimodal Deep Learning, MIMIC-CXR, Bio\_ClinicalBERT,
Cross-Attention, WBC Fusion, Pneumonia Detection.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Pneumonia Detection, CXR, Radiology Text, WBC, Multimodal Fusion, MIMIC-CXR
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
% ==========================================================

Pneumonia causes $\sim$2.5 million deaths annually~\cite{who2023}.
Chest radiography (CXR) is the primary diagnostic tool, yet visual ambiguity
means even experienced radiologists disagree on 20--30\% of borderline cases.
AI systems trained on images alone hit a performance ceiling because the image
cannot capture the patient's \emph{systemic} response to infection.
Two additional sources of clinical information are available at the bedside:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Radiology reports}: the radiologist's free-text description
    of FINDINGS (what is seen) and HISTORY (why the patient was imaged).
  \item \textbf{WBC count}: from a routine Complete Blood Count (CBC),
    available within 15 minutes of ED admission---the canonical infection biomarker.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=red!4!white,colframe=red!55!black,
  title=Anti-Leakage Protocol,fonttitle=\small\bfseries,
  top=3pt,bottom=3pt,left=4pt,right=4pt]
\small
Every radiology report ends with \textbf{IMPRESSION} stating the diagnosis.
If the model sees this, it reads the answer---not a real AI.\\
\textcolor{successgrn}{\checkmark}~\textbf{FINDINGS + HISTORY} --- retained.\\
\textcolor{alertred}{\textbf{$\times$}~\textbf{IMPRESSION}} --- stripped completely.
\end{tcolorbox}

Part~2 builds a three-stage progressive fusion pipeline on
MIMIC-CXR-JPG~v2.0.0 (PhysioNet DUA + CITI required), adding one modality
at a time to enable clean ablation:
Phase~1 (image only) $\to$ Phase~2 (image\,+\,text) $\to$
Phase~3c (image\,+\,text\,+\,WBC).

\section{Dataset}
% ==========================================================

All experiments use \textbf{MIMIC-CXR-JPG~v2.0.0}~\cite{johnson2019mimic}
(Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Centre, 2011--2016).
Images are filtered to \textbf{PA~(Posteroanterior) view only}---the
standard diagnostic frontal view---to eliminate the AP/Lateral confound
identified in Part~1.
WBC counts are extracted from \textbf{MIMIC-IV}~\cite{johnson2023mimiciv}
\texttt{labevents} and linked to each CXR study by \texttt{subject\_id}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\caption{Scale-up dataset used for all Part~2 experiments.}
\label{tab:dataset}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
\textbf{Property} & \textbf{Value}\\
\midrule
Total images     & 3,763 (PA-only)\\
Normal           & 1,872 \quad Pneumonia: 1,891\\
Balance          & 1:1 (stratified by patient)\\
Split (patient-level) & Train 2,633 / Val 565 / Test 565\\
Text input       & FINDINGS + HISTORY (IMPRESSION stripped)\\
WBC coverage     & 100\% (imputed with train-median where missing)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Anti-leakage split}: \texttt{GroupShuffleSplit} by \texttt{subject\_id}
guarantees zero patient overlap across train, validation, and test sets.
Missing WBC values are filled with the \emph{training-set median only};
the same median is applied to validation and test---no test information
leaks into imputation.

\section{Architecture}
% ==========================================================

\subsection{Phase~1 — Image Only (Baseline)}

DenseNet-121~\cite{huang2017densenet} pretrained on $>$500K CXRs
(\texttt{torchxrayvision}~\cite{cohen2022torchxrayvision},
\texttt{densenet121-res224-all} weights) is augmented with a
\textbf{CBAM}~\cite{woo2018cbam} (Channel + Spatial Attention) module
and trained with 5-fold GroupKFold cross-validation, TTA (10 views),
and Focal Loss~\cite{lin2017focal}.
The Phase~1 best-fold encoder (1024-d feature vector, \textbf{frozen}) is
reused in all subsequent phases.

\subsection{Phase~2 — Image + Radiology Text}

Bio\_ClinicalBERT~\cite{alsentzer2019bert} (pretrained on MIMIC-III notes)
encodes the leakage-free report text into a sequence of 768-d token embeddings.
The last 2 BERT layers are fine-tuned at \texttt{lr=1e-5} to adapt
to the pneumonia classification task without catastrophic forgetting.
An 8-head \textbf{cross-attention} module lets the frozen image features
\emph{query} the full BERT token sequence:
\vspace{-2pt}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{c} = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}_{\mathrm{img}}\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{txt}}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{txt}},
  \quad d_k = 64 \;(512\text{ heads}/8)
  \label{eq:crossattn}
\end{equation}
The attended text vector $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$ captures the
specific report tokens most relevant to the image features, rather than
relying on a single \texttt{[CLS]} summary token.
Training uses Focal Loss~\cite{lin2017focal} ($\gamma$\,=\,2.0) and
Mixup~\cite{zhang2018mixup} on embeddings ($\alpha$\,=\,0.2).

\label{fig:p3c}

Phase~3c adds a \textbf{WBC branch} on top of the Phase~2 cross-attention backbone.
A two-hidden-layer MLP (\texttt{WBCMetadataEncoder}: $1\!\to\!128\!\to\!128\!\to\!64$)
projects the scalar WBC count into a 64-d embedding.
The three branch outputs are concatenated:
\vspace{-2pt}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z} = [\,\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{img}} \,\|\, \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{cross}} \,\|\,
  \mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{wbc}}\,] \in \mathbb{R}^{1600}
  \label{eq:p3c}
\end{equation}
The Phase~2v2 cross-attention weights are \textbf{warm-started} into Phase~3c,
preserving the learned image-text alignment. Only the WBC encoder and fusion
MLP are initialised from scratch.
Training uses Focal Loss ($\gamma$\,=\,2.0) and triple-branch
Mixup~\cite{zhang2018mixup} ($\alpha$\,=\,0.2) with separate learning rates:
BERT $=$ 1e-5, fusion/WBC $=$ 2e-4 / 1e-3.

\textbf{Conclusion}: Phase~3c closes 92.5\% of the performance gap
at a fraction of the clinical data cost.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{Discussion}
% ==========================================================

\subsection{Why Does WBC Help Beyond Text?}

Radiology FINDINGS describe \emph{what the radiologist sees} on the image.
WBC measures the patient's \emph{systemic immune response}---information
that is invisible in any image. A consolidation on the CXR could be
pneumonia, atelectasis, or pulmonary oedema; an elevated WBC ($>$11\,000/\textmu{}L)
decisively favours infectious aetiology.
The cross-attention mechanism already extracts the ``is there opacity?'' signal
from image+text; WBC adds the ``is the body fighting an infection?'' signal,
which is why sensitivity improves most (+5.65\%): WBC confirms the cases
the image-text model was uncertain about.

\subsection{WBC Embedding: Why an MLP over a Scalar?}

A na{\"i}ve approach would concatenate the raw WBC scalar directly.
Instead, the \texttt{WBCMetadataEncoder} ($1\!\to\!128\!\to\!128\!\to\!64$)
projects it into a 64-d embedding. This allows the model to learn
\emph{non-linear} thresholds: e.g., WBC\,=\,12 vs.\ 15 vs.\ 25 have
qualitatively different clinical meanings (borderline / elevated / severely elevated),
which a scalar concatenation cannot capture but an MLP can.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Single institution}: MIMIC-CXR is from one hospital (BIDMC).
        Results may not generalise to hospitals with different scanner
        protocols, patient demographics, or reporting styles.
  \item \textbf{WBC imputation}: $\sim$15\% of WBC values were imputed
        with training-set median. Imputed samples reduce the model's
        advantage over text-only.
  \item \textbf{No external validation}: the model has not been tested
        on NIH ChestX-14 or CheXpert.
  \item \textbf{Binary classification}: real clinical CXR reads distinguish
        14+ pathologies; this model is pneumonia-vs-normal only.
  \item \textbf{Data access}: MIMIC-CXR requires PhysioNet DUA + CITI
        training. No data is redistributed with this report.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
% ==========================================================

This report demonstrates that a \textbf{minimal triple-fusion model}
(CXR image + leakage-free radiology text + WBC count) achieves strong
clinical performance on MIMIC-CXR:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\caption{Final Phase~3c results summary.}
\label{tab:final}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Phase~3c Value}\\
\midrule
AUC-ROC         & \textbf{0.9711}\\
Sensitivity (Youden) & \textbf{92.25\%}\\
Specificity (Youden) & \textbf{93.59\%}\\
Accuracy (Youden)    & \textbf{92.92\%}\\
Test set size        & 565 patients (balanced)\\
Dataset              & MIMIC-CXR PA, 3,763 images\\
Extra data needed    & 1 lab value (WBC from routine CBC)\\
\bottomrule

The key finding is that \textbf{WBC is the single most impactful lab value}
to add to an image+text model: it closes 92.5\% of the performance gap
between text-only (AUC\,=\,0.946) and the full 17-feature system (AUC\,=\,0.989),
while remaining available in any ED within 15 minutes.
The full experimental journey --- from image-only AUC\,=\,0.826
to image+text+WBC AUC\,=\,0.971 --- demonstrates that each modality
adds complementary, medically interpretable signal.
