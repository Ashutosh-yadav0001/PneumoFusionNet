\begin{abstract}
\begin{tcolorbox}[
  colback=aisoft!70,
  colframe=aideep,
  arc=3pt,
  boxrule=0.8pt,
  left=5pt,right=5pt,top=5pt,bottom=5pt
]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{aideep}{Clinical Problem:}} \textbf{Chest radiography (CXR) interpretation is constrained by severe visual overlap with atelectasis and non-infectious opacities, while imaging alone cannot quantify host systemic inflammation.}

\vspace{3pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{aideep}{Multimodal Approach:}} \textbf{We present PneumoFusionNet, a progressive multimodal framework evaluated on 3{,}763 frontal posteroanterior (PA) radiographs from MIMIC-CXR coupled with clinical notes from MIMIC-III and laboratory records from MIMIC-IV. The framework integrates a TorchXRayVision DenseNet-121 backbone augmented with a Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), Bio\_ClinicalBERT token representations fused via 8-head cross-attention, and a non-linear White Blood Cell (WBC) encoder MLP ($1 \to 128 \to 128 \to 64$).}

\vspace{3pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{aideep}{Anti-Leakage Safeguards:}} \textbf{All diagnostic IMPRESSION sections are stripped before tokenisation and trigger keywords are redacted, ensuring zero label memorisation. Patient-level group splits guarantee zero patient overlap across all partitions.}

\vspace{3pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{aideep}{Key Results:}}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=1pt,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{CXR Baseline (Phase~1):} AUC\,=\,0.826, Sensitivity\,=\,71.0\% (5-fold GroupKFold, $N$\,=\,3{,}763).
  \item \textbf{Image + Text (Phase~2v2):} AUC\,=\,0.946, Accuracy\,=\,87.8\% (+12.0~pp over Phase~1).
  \item \textbf{Triple Fusion (Phase~3c, Main Model):} AUC\,=\,0.971, Sensitivity\,=\,92.25\%, Specificity\,=\,93.59\% at Youden-J threshold ($\tau\!=\!0.559$). Attains 94.72\% sensitivity at emergency screening threshold ($\tau\!=\!0.500$).
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}
\end{abstract}

% ==========================================================
%  I. INTRODUCTION
% ==========================================================
\section{\textcolor{aideep}{Introduction}}

Pneumonia remains the leading infectious cause of mortality globally, accounting for approximately 2.5~million deaths annually~\cite{gbd2019}, and is the single largest infectious killer of children under five~\cite{who2023}. Chest radiography (CXR) is the primary screening modality in both high- and low-resource clinical settings; however, solo radiographic interpretation is inherently difficult. Consolidation, ground-glass opacities, and interstitial infiltrates, which represent the hallmark radiological features of pneumonia, overlap considerably with atelectasis, pleural effusion, pulmonary oedema, and neoplastic disease~\cite{kelly2012}.

\textbf{Deep learning for CXR analysis.} CheXNet~\cite{rajpurkar2017chexnet} demonstrated that a 121-layer DenseNet trained on the NIH ChestX-ray14 dataset could match radiologist-level pneumonia detection performance, achieving an AUC of 0.768 on the pneumonia class, galvanising interest in CNN-based clinical decision support. Guan~et~al.~\cite{guan2018multi} showed multi-label CXR classification benefits from self-supervised attention guided by multiple pathology categories simultaneously. Wang~et~al.~\cite{wang2017chestxray14} released the ChestX-ray14 benchmark enabling comparison across 14 thoracic pathologies and establishing AUC as the canonical evaluation metric. Despite these advances, pure vision models exhibit a performance ceiling because X-rays encode anatomy but not physiology.

\textbf{Multimodal radiology AI.} Physicians do not interpret images in isolation: they combine visual findings with clinical history, radiology report narratives, and laboratory biomarkers. Alsentzer~et~al.~\cite{alsentzer2019bert} released Bio\_ClinicalBERT, a BERT variant pre-trained on MIMIC-III discharge summaries, providing domain-adapted language representations that capture medical terminology far better than general-purpose language models. Zhang~et~al.~\cite{zhang2020contrastive} applied contrastive learning to jointly represent CXR images and associated report sentences, improving zero-shot transfer across thoracic pathologies. However, a critical weakness in most multimodal systems is \emph{label leakage}: radiology reports contain IMPRESSION sections that directly state the radiologist's diagnosis, and models trained on raw reports learn to read the diagnosis rather than to infer it from evidence~\cite{bannur2023knowledge}.

\textbf{Laboratory biomarkers as a complementary signal.} The White Blood Cell (WBC) count from a routine Complete Blood Count (CBC) is one of the oldest and most universally available inflammatory markers. Elevated WBC (leukocytosis, $>$11{,}000~cells/$\mu$L) is a hallmark of bacterial infection and is routinely ordered in emergency triage. Critically, CBC results are available within 15~minutes of a blood draw, fitting naturally into the initial 30-minute emergency admission window alongside the chest radiograph. To date, no published system has specifically studied the marginal value of WBC count as a targeted, minimal-overhead complement to CXR + radiology text under strict leakage controls on a large credentialled dataset.

\textbf{Contributions.} This paper presents \textbf{PneumoFusionNet}, which makes the following specific contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, leftmargin=*]
  \item A \textbf{leakage-controlled text pipeline} that enforces IMPRESSION removal and keyword redaction, validated by a sanity check showing performance degrades to AUC\,=\,0.512 when only the stripped IMPRESSION text is provided.
  \item A \textbf{progressive three-phase architecture}: image-only (Phase~1) $\to$ image+text cross-attention (Phase~2v2) $\to$ image+text+WBC triple fusion (Phase~3c), enabling quantitative isolation of each modality's contribution.
  \item Comprehensive evaluation on 3{,}763 PA-view MIMIC-CXR studies with zero-patient-overlap splits, multiple threshold strategies calibrated to clinical deployment scenarios (emergency triage vs.\ confirmatory testing), and comparison against published state-of-the-art systems.
\end{enumerate}

% ==========================================================
%  II. RELATED WORK
% ==========================================================
\section{\textcolor{aideep}{Related Work}}

\textbf{Image-only CXR classifiers.} Following CheXNet~\cite{rajpurkar2017chexnet}, numerous architectures have been applied to thoracic disease detection. EfficientNet-based models~\cite{tan2019efficientnet} achieve strong ImageNet transfer but underperform domain-pretrained models on grayscale radiographs. In our pilot experiments, replacing TorchXRayVision DenseNet-121 with an ImageNet-pretrained backbone resulted in a notable drop in AUC. Attention mechanisms such as CBAM~\cite{woo2018cbam} and spatial pyramid pooling have been shown to improve localisation of pulmonary infiltrates.

\textbf{Multimodal image-text systems.} ConVIRT~\cite{zhang2020contrastive} and BioViL~\cite{bannur2023knowledge} use contrastive learning to align CXR embeddings with report sentences, demonstrating substantial zero-shot transfer. These are pre-training frameworks rather than supervised classifiers. Our Phase~2v2 builds on the supervised cross-attention paradigm, extending it with strict leakage controls not present in prior work.

\textbf{Clinical metadata integration.} Yu~et~al.~\cite{frontiers2025pneumofusion} proposed PneumoFusion-Net combining ResNet-50 with Global Channel-Spatial Attention for pneumonia classification, achieving strong multi-class performance on CT imaging. Acosta~et~al.~\cite{acosta2022multimodal} demonstrated that combining imaging with tabular EHR data in a multimodal transformer substantially outperforms either modality alone for mortality prediction. Our Phase~3c study is unique in isolating the marginal value of a single, rapidly-available biomarker (WBC) rather than a large feature panel, and comparing this targeted fusion against the full 17-feature EHR variant on the restricted MIMIC-CXR clinical dataset.

% ==========================================================
%  III. DATASET
% ==========================================================
\section{\textcolor{aideep}{Dataset}}

Experiments used \textbf{MIMIC-CXR-JPG v2.0.0}~\cite{johnson2019mimic}, a de-identified clinical chest X-ray dataset from Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Centre (BIDMC), paired with free-text radiology reports. Only \textbf{posteroanterior (PA)} radiographs were retained to eliminate view-acquisition bias: pilot experiments showed that AP views correlate with patient acuity (bedside ICU imaging) rather than pathology, creating an acquisition-bias shortcut that inflates test AUC.

White Blood Cell measurements were extracted from \textbf{MIMIC-IV}~\cite{johnson2023mimiciv} laboratory records (ItemIDs 51300/51301 in \texttt{labevents}) and linked to radiographic studies via patient identifiers and study date proximity ($\pm$2~days). When multiple WBC readings existed within the window, the temporally closest measurement to the study date was selected.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{Dataset Composition:}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Normal}: 1{,}872 studies \quad \textbf{Pneumonia}: 1{,}891 studies \quad \textbf{Total}: 3{,}763
  \item \textbf{Patient-level split}: Train\,2{,}633 / Val\,565 / Test\,565
  \item Approximately 15\% of WBC values were missing; imputed using the median computed exclusively from the training partition
  \item \textbf{WBC clinical signal}: Pneumonia cases show 15.4\% leukocytosis rate vs.\ 7.2\% in normal cases, confirming discriminatory value of the biomarker
\end{itemize}

\section{\textcolor{aideep}{Methodology}}

\subsection{Phase~1: Vision Backbone (Image-Only Baseline)}

The image encoder uses \textbf{DenseNet-121}~\cite{huang2017densenet} pre-trained on hundreds of thousands of frontal chest radiographs via the TorchXRayVision~\cite{cohen2022torchxrayvision} library, which aggregates multiple public chest X-ray datasets (MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert, PadChest, NIH ChestX-ray14, and others). Domain-specific pre-training is critical: pilot experiments showed that replacing this backbone with an ImageNet-pretrained model caused a significant AUC drop on the scaled PA cohort, confirming the necessity of radiological domain transfer.

Images are normalised to the TorchXRayVision intensity range $[-1024, 1024]$, resized to $224 \times 224$, and preprocessed with contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalisation (\textbf{CLAHE}, clip limit 2.0, tile grid $8 \times 8$) to enhance subtle infiltrate contrast. Training augmentation includes random horizontal flip, rotation ($\pm10°$), small translation ($\pm5\%$), and Gaussian blur.

A \textbf{Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)}~\cite{woo2018cbam} is appended after the DenseNet backbone to direct the network toward diagnostically relevant pulmonary regions:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}' = \mathbf{M}_c(\mathbf{F}) \otimes \mathbf{F}, \quad \mathbf{F}'' = \mathbf{M}_s(\mathbf{F}') \otimes \mathbf{F}'
\end{equation}
where the channel attention map is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{M}_c(\mathbf{F}) = \sigma\!\left(\text{MLP}\!\left(\text{AvgPool}(\mathbf{F})\right) + \text{MLP}\!\left(\text{MaxPool}(\mathbf{F})\right)\right)
  \label{eq:channel}
\end{equation}
with channel reduction ratio $r\!=\!16$, and the spatial attention map is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{M}_s(\mathbf{F}') = \sigma\!\left(f^{7\times7}\!\left([\text{AvgPool}_c(\mathbf{F}');\,\text{MaxPool}_c(\mathbf{F}')]\right)\right)
  \label{eq:spatial}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function and $f^{7\times7}$ is a $7\times7$ convolutional filter.

\textbf{Training:} 5-fold GroupKFold cross-validation (grouped on \texttt{subject\_id}) with zero patient overlap, \textbf{Focal Loss}~\cite{lin2017focal} ($\gamma\!=\!2.0$), AdamW ($\eta\!=\!10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), cosine annealing LR schedule, and 10-view \textbf{Test-Time Augmentation} (TTA). The encoder output is a \textbf{1024-dimensional} global average pooled feature vector $\mathbf{v}_{\text{img}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$, frozen for all downstream phases.

\subsection{Phase~2: Radiology Text Fusion via Cross-Attention}

\textbf{Anti-leakage preprocessing} (Section~\ref{sec:antileak}) strips diagnostic IMPRESSION/CONCLUSION sections and redacts explicit pathology keywords. Cleaned text is truncated/padded to 256 WordPiece tokens.

Text is encoded using \textbf{Bio\_ClinicalBERT}~\cite{alsentzer2019bert}, a 110M-parameter BERT-base model pre-trained on MIMIC-III clinical notes. The \textbf{top two transformer layers (10 and 11)} are unfrozen with learning rate $\eta_{\text{BERT}}\!=\!10^{-5}$; all lower layers remain frozen. The encoder emits the full token sequence $\mathbf{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 256 \times 768}$.

\textbf{8-head cross-attention fusion.} Image features act as queries; textual tokens act as keys and values, allowing the model to identify which textual evidence is most relevant to the visual finding:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{c}_{\text{cross}} = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}_{\text{img}}\mathbf{K}_{\text{txt}}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf{V}_{\text{txt}}, \quad d_k = 64
  \label{eq:crossattn}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{Q}_{\text{img}} = \mathbf{v}_{\text{img}}\mathbf{W}_Q$ ($\mathbf{W}_Q \in \mathbb{R}^{1024 \times 512}$), and $\mathbf{K}_{\text{txt}}, \mathbf{V}_{\text{txt}}$ project text tokens via $\mathbf{W}_K, \mathbf{W}_V \in \mathbb{R}^{768 \times 512}$. After multi-head pooling and linear projection: $\mathbf{c}_{\text{cross}} \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$.

The classification head for Phase~2:
\begin{equation}
  [\mathbf{v}_{\text{img}} \,\|\, \mathbf{c}_{\text{cross}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{1536} \xrightarrow{\text{LN} \to 512 \to 128 \to 2} \hat{y}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Training:} Focal Loss ($\gamma\!=\!2.0$), embedding Mixup~\cite{zhang2018mixup} ($\alpha\!=\!0.2$), differential AdamW: fusion head at $2\!\times\!10^{-4}$, BERT at $10^{-5}$. Early stopping on validation AUC (patience~8). \textbf{Trainable parameters: $\approx$\,18M}.

\subsection{Phase~3c: WBC Triple Fusion (Main Conference Model)}

The WBC scalar is standardised using a \texttt{StandardScaler} fitted on the training split and projected through a dedicated non-linear encoder:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{e}_{\text{wbc}} = \text{MLP}(\text{wbc}): \; 1 \to 128 \to 128 \to 64
  \label{eq:wbcmlp}
\end{equation}
with ReLU activations and dropout $(p\!=\!0.3, p\!=\!0.2)$. This nonlinear projection is clinically motivated: a WBC of 12{,}000 vs.\ 16{,}000 vs.\ 28{,}000~cells/$\mu$L represents qualitatively distinct physiological states that a linear transformation cannot adequately capture.

All three embeddings are concatenated:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z} = [\,\mathbf{v}_{\text{img}} \,\|\, \mathbf{c}_{\text{cross}} \,\|\, \mathbf{e}_{\text{wbc}}\,] \in \mathbb{R}^{1600}
  \label{eq:concat}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}_{\text{img}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$, $\mathbf{c}_{\text{cross}} \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$, $\mathbf{e}_{\text{wbc}} \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$.

The classifier head:
\begin{equation}
  \text{LN}(1600) \to \text{Drop}(0.4) \to 512 \to \text{Drop}(0.3) \to 128 \to \text{Drop}(0.2) \to 2
  \label{eq:head}
\end{equation}
with GELU activations throughout.

\textbf{Warm-start:} Phase~3c loads the trained Phase~2v2 checkpoint, preserving cross-attention alignment while initialising the WBC encoder from random weights. Differential learning rates: BERT\,=\,$10^{-5}$, fusion head\,=\,$2\!\times\!10^{-4}$, WBC encoder\,=\,$10^{-3}$. \textbf{Total trainable parameters: $\approx$\,18M.}

\textbf{Threshold calibration:} Decision thresholds are selected on the validation set using Youden's J statistic ($J = \text{TPR} - \text{FPR}$) for balanced operation and $\tau\!=\!0.500$ for emergency screening. The held-out test set is never used for threshold selection.

% ==========================================================
%  V. ANTI-LEAKAGE PROTOCOL
% ==========================================================
\section{\textcolor{aideep}{Anti-Leakage Protocol}}
\label{sec:antileak}

Radiology reports contain an IMPRESSION section that directly states the radiologist's diagnosis. Models trained on raw reports achieve artificially high performance by memorising this conclusion rather than learning from clinical evidence~\cite{bannur2023knowledge}. PneumoFusionNet enforces the following safeguards:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Impression Removal:} IMPRESSION and CONCLUSION sections are stripped via regular-expression parsing. Only FINDINGS and HISTORY are retained.
  \item \textbf{Keyword Redaction:} Diagnostic trigger words (e.g., ``pneumonia'', ``lobar consolidation'', ``compatible with infection'', ``no acute cardiopulmonary process'') are replaced with \texttt{[REDACTED]}.
  \item \textbf{Patient-Level Splitting:} All partitions enforce strict grouping on \texttt{subject\_id} using GroupKFold (Phase~1) and GroupShuffleSplit (Phases~2--3c). Verified: Train~$\cap$~Val\,=\,$\emptyset$, Train~$\cap$~Test\,=\,$\emptyset$, Val~$\cap$~Test\,=\,$\emptyset$.
  \item \textbf{PA-Only Filtering:} AP views removed after pilot experiments revealed view-type correlation with patient acuity.
  \item \textbf{Train-Only Imputation:} Missing WBC values (15\%) imputed using training-set median only.
  \item \textbf{Leakage Sanity Check:} A model trained on only the stripped IMPRESSION text achieved AUC\,=\,0.512, confirming no residual diagnostic signal.
\end{enumerate}

%  VII. DISCUSSION
% ==========================================================
\section{\textcolor{aideep}{Discussion}}

\textbf{Multimodal learning substantially outperforms image-only approaches.} The image-only model plateaued at AUC\,=\,0.826 despite extensive architectural optimisation. This ceiling reflects a fundamental limitation: chest radiographs encode morphological structure but not physiological state, such as the duration and severity of systemic inflammation, host immune response, or pre-existing comorbidities.

\textbf{Cross-attention enables selective image-text alignment.} The 8-head cross-attention mechanism allows image features to selectively attend to relevant textual tokens. When the radiograph shows an ambiguous opacity, the model attends to descriptive terms such as ``consolidation'', ``fever'', or ``productive cough'' in the FINDINGS section, disambiguating pathology from benign atelectasis or effusion.

\textbf{Strict anti-leakage is essential for valid evaluation.} Removing the IMPRESSION section and redacting diagnostic keywords ensures that observed performance reflects genuine multimodal learning. Our sanity check, where training on stripped IMPRESSION text alone achieved AUC\,=\,0.512, confirms the protocol's effectiveness.

\textbf{A single biomarker provides substantial diagnostic value.} Although WBC is only one scalar measurement, its integration over image-text fusion contributed meaningful improvements: +2.5~pp AUC, +5.6~pp sensitivity, and +4.5~pp specificity. A Complete Blood Count is universally available and results arrive within 15~minutes of admission, making the CXR\,+\,Report\,+\,WBC triad feasible within 30~minutes of emergency presentation.

\textbf{Comparison with full clinical panel.} The 17-feature full EHR panel (Phase~3 Full, AUC\,=\,0.969) delivered lower sensitivity (89.1\% vs.\ 92.3\%) and required 1--4~hours of laboratory processing. Phase~3c with WBC alone achieves better sensitivity with a dramatically faster turnaround, suggesting that targeted single-biomarker fusion is preferable to comprehensive but delayed panel integration.

\textbf{Limitations.} All data originate from a single US institution (BIDMC). The binary classification formulation does not address multi-label co-morbidities. Approximately 15\% of WBC values required median imputation. External validation on diverse populations and scanner hardware is necessary before clinical deployment.

% ==========================================================
%  VIII. CONCLUSION
% ==========================================================
\section{\textcolor{aideep}{Conclusion}}

We presented PneumoFusionNet, a leakage-controlled progressive multimodal framework that integrates chest radiographs, anti-leakage radiology report text, and White Blood Cell count for pneumonia detection on 3{,}763 MIMIC-CXR studies. The proposed approach achieved systematic improvements from image-only learning (AUC\,=\,0.826) through image-text cross-attention fusion (AUC\,=\,0.946) to image-text-WBC triple fusion (AUC\,=\,0.971, Sensitivity\,=\,92.3\%, Specificity\,=\,93.6\%). Phase~3c outperforms the full 17-feature EHR panel in sensitivity while using a single, rapidly-available laboratory biomarker, and the entire CXR\,+\,Report\,+\,WBC triad can be evaluated within 30~minutes of emergency admission. Future work will investigate external validation on geographically diverse hospital datasets, multi-label pathology classification, and additional laboratory biomarkers (CRP, procalcitonin) as complementary inflammatory markers.
