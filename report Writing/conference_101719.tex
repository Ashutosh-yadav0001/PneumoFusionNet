\begin{abstract}
\begin{tcolorbox}[
  colback=aisoft!70,
  colframe=aideep,
  arc=3pt,
  boxrule=0.8pt,
  left=5pt,right=5pt,top=5pt,bottom=5pt
]
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{aideep}{Clinical Problem:}} \textbf{Chest radiography (CXR) interpretation is constrained by severe visual overlap with atelectasis and non-infectious opacities, while imaging alone cannot quantify host systemic inflammation.}

\vspace{3pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{aideep}{Multimodal Approach:}} \textbf{We present PneumoFusionNet, a progressive multimodal framework evaluated on 3{,}763 frontal posteroanterior (PA) radiographs from MIMIC-CXR coupled with clinical notes from MIMIC-III and laboratory records from MIMIC-IV. The framework integrates a TorchXRayVision DenseNet-121 backbone augmented with a Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), Bio\_ClinicalBERT token representations fused via 8-head cross-attention, and a non-linear White Blood Cell (WBC) encoder MLP ($1 \to 128 \to 128 \to 64$). Additionally, a lightweight real-world deployment variant (Phase~4a) uses Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) to condition image features directly on the WBC scalar without requiring radiology text.}

\vspace{3pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{aideep}{Anti-Leakage Safeguards:}} \textbf{All diagnostic IMPRESSION sections are stripped before tokenisation and trigger keywords are redacted, ensuring zero label memorisation. Patient-level group splits guarantee zero patient overlap across all partitions.}

\vspace{3pt}
\noindent\textbf{\textcolor{aideep}{Key Results:}}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=1pt,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{CXR Baseline (Phase~1):} AUC\,=\,0.826, Sensitivity\,=\,71.0\% (5-fold GroupKFold, $N$\,=\,3{,}763).
  \item \textbf{Image + Text (Phase~2v2):} AUC\,=\,0.946, Accuracy\,=\,87.8\% (+12.0~pp over Phase~1).
  \item \textbf{Real-World Variant (Phase~4a, CXR + WBC only):} AUC\,=\,0.895, Sensitivity\,=\,84.5\%, deployable in \textless{}15~min with no radiology report required.
  \item \textbf{Triple Fusion (Phase~3c, Main Model):} AUC\,=\,0.971, Sensitivity\,=\,92.25\%, Specificity\,=\,93.59\% at Youden-J threshold ($\tau\!=\!0.559$). Attains 94.72\% sensitivity at emergency screening threshold ($\tau\!=\!0.500$).
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}
\end{abstract}

% ==========================================================
%  I. INTRODUCTION
% ==========================================================
\section{\textcolor{aideep}{Introduction}}

Pneumonia kills approximately 2.5~million people annually and is the leading infectious cause of mortality in children under five~\cite{who2023}. Chest radiography (CXR) is the primary screening modality in both high- and low-resource clinical settings; however, solo radiographic interpretation is inherently difficult. Consolidation, ground-glass opacities, and interstitial infiltrates—the radiological hallmarks of pneumonia—overlap considerably with atelectasis, pleural effusion, pulmonary oedema, and neoplastic disease, producing false-positive and false-negative rates that limit diagnostic confidence~\cite{kelly2012}.

\textbf{Deep learning for CXR analysis.} CheXNet~\cite{rajpurkar2017chexnet} demonstrated that a 121-layer DenseNet trained on CheXpert data could match radiologist-level performance on pneumonia detection, galvanising interest in CNN-based clinical decision support. Subsequent works introduced attention mechanisms: Guan~et~al.~\cite{guan2018multi} showed multi-label CXR classification benefits from self-supervised attention guided by multiple categories simultaneously. Wang~et~al.~\cite{wang2017chestxray14} released the large-scale ChestX-ray14 benchmark, enabling comparison across 14 thoracic pathologies and establishing AUC as the canonical evaluation metric. Despite these advances, pure vision models exhibit a performance ceiling because X-rays encode anatomy but not physiology.

\textbf{Multimodal radiology AI.} Physicians do not interpret images in isolation: they combine visual findings with clinical history, radiology report narratives, and laboratory biomarkers. Alsentzer~et~al.~\cite{alsentzer2019bert} released Bio\_ClinicalBERT, a BERT variant pre-trained on MIMIC-III discharge summaries and clinical notes, providing domain-adapted language representations that capture medical terminology far better than general-purpose language models. Zhang~et~al.~\cite{zhang2020contrastive} applied contrastive learning to jointly represent CXR images and associated report sentences, improving zero-shot transfer across thoracic pathologies. Moon~et~al.~\cite{moon2022multimodal} proposed a multi-task transformer that jointly encodes image, report text, and structured metadata for comprehensive radiology report generation. However, a critical weakness in most multimodal systems is \emph{label leakage}: radiology reports contain IMPRESSION sections that directly state the radiologist's diagnosis, and models trained on raw reports learn to read the diagnosis rather than to infer it from evidence~\cite{bannur2023knowledge}.

\textbf{Laboratory biomarkers as a complementary signal.} The White Blood Cell (WBC) count from a routine Complete Blood Count (CBC) is one of the oldest and most universally available inflammatory markers. Elevated WBC (leukocytosis, $>$11{,}000~cells/$\mu$L) is a hallmark of bacterial infection and is routinely ordered in emergency triage for every patient with suspected pneumonia. Critically, CBC results are available within 15~minutes of a blood draw, fitting naturally into the initial 30-minute emergency admission window alongside the chest radiograph. Yu~et~al.~\cite{frontiers2025pneumofusion} integrated multi-modal signals including imaging with clinical metadata in PneumoFusion-Net using a ResNet-50 backbone with Global Channel-Spatial Attention, achieving improvements over single-modality approaches; however, they did not enforce anti-leakage protocols for clinical text. To date, no published system has specifically studied the marginal value of WBC count as a targeted, minimal-overhead complement to CXR + radiology text under strict leakage controls on a large credentialled dataset.

\textbf{Contributions.} This paper presents \textbf{PneumoFusionNet}, which makes the following specific contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, leftmargin=*]
  \item A \textbf{leakage-controlled text pipeline} that enforces IMPRESSION removal and keyword redaction, validated by observing that model performance degrades to random if only redacted IMPRESSION text is provided.
  \item A \textbf{progressive three-phase architecture}: image-only (Phase~1) $\to$ image+text cross-attention (Phase~2v2) $\to$ image+text+WBC triple fusion (Phase~3c), enabling quantitative isolation of each modality's contribution.
  \item \textbf{Phase~4a}: a lightweight \textbf{FiLM-conditioned dual-fusion} model (CXR + WBC only, no report needed), designed for settings where radiology reports are unavailable or delayed, achieving AUC\,=\,0.895 with $<$1M trainable parameters.
  \item Comprehensive evaluation on 3{,}763 PA-view MIMIC-CXR studies with zero-patient-overlap splits, multiple threshold strategies calibrated to clinical deployment scenarios, and comparison against published state-of-the-art systems.
\end{enumerate}

% ==========================================================
%  II. RELATED WORK
% ==========================================================
\section{\textcolor{aideep}{Related Work}}

\textbf{Image-only CXR classifiers.} Following CheXNet~\cite{rajpurkar2017chexnet}, numerous architectures have been applied to thoracic disease detection. EfficientNet-based models~\cite{tan2019efficientnet} achieve strong ImageNet transfer but underperform domain-pretrained models on grayscale radiographs---our pilot experiments confirmed an AUC drop from 0.826 to 0.713 when replacing TorchXRayVision DenseNet-121 with an ImageNet EfficientNet-B4. Attention mechanisms such as CBAM~\cite{woo2018cbam} and spatial pyramid pooling have been shown to improve localisation of pulmonary infiltrates.

\textbf{Multimodal image-text systems.} ConVIRT~\cite{zhang2020contrastive} and BioViL~\cite{bannur2023knowledge} use contrastive learning to align CXR embeddings with report sentences, demonstrating substantial zero-shot transfer. However, these systems require paired image-report data and are pre-training frameworks rather than supervised classifiers. Our Phase~2v2 builds on the supervised cross-attention paradigm of~\cite{moon2022multimodal}, extending it with strict leakage controls not present in prior work.

\textbf{Clinical metadata integration.} CheXFusion~\cite{kovaleva2020towards} fused structured metadata (age, gender, view position) with image features via concatenation, achieving modest gains. More recent work by~\cite{acosta2022multimodal} demonstrated that combining imaging with tabular EHR data in a multimodal transformer substantially outperforms either modality alone for mortality prediction. Our Phase~3c study is unique in isolating the marginal value of a single, rapidly-available biomarker (WBC) rather than a large feature panel, and in comparing this targeted fusion against the full 17-feature EHR variant.

\textbf{FiLM conditioning for medical imaging.} Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)~\cite{perez2018film} was introduced for visual reasoning tasks where a language conditioning signal modulates intermediate CNN representations. Dumoulin~et~al.~\cite{dumoulin2017learned} demonstrated FiLM-style normalisation in style transfer. Recent medical applications have used FiLM to condition image segmentation networks on patient metadata~\cite{lemay2021benefits}, motivating our Phase~4a design.

% ==========================================================
%  III. DATASET
% ==========================================================
\section{\textcolor{aideep}{Dataset}}

Experiments used \textbf{MIMIC-CXR-JPG v2.0.0}~\cite{johnson2019mimic}, a de-identified clinical chest X-ray dataset from Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Centre (BIDMC), paired with free-text radiology reports. Only \textbf{posteroanterior (PA)} radiographs were retained to eliminate view-acquisition bias: pilot experiments showed that AP views correlate with patient acuity (bedside ICU imaging) rather than pathology, creating an acquisition-bias shortcut that inflates test AUC.

White Blood Cell measurements were extracted from \textbf{MIMIC-IV}~\cite{johnson2023mimiciv} laboratory records (ItemIDs 51300/51301 in \texttt{labevents}) and linked to radiographic studies via patient identifiers and study date proximity ($\pm$2~days). When multiple WBC readings existed within the window, the temporally closest measurement to the study date was selected.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{Dataset Composition:}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Normal}: 1{,}872 studies \quad \textbf{Pneumonia}: 1{,}891 studies \quad \textbf{Total}: 3{,}763
  \item \textbf{Patient-level split}: Train\,2{,}633 / Val\,565 / Test\,565
  \item Approximately 15\% of WBC values were missing; imputed using the median computed exclusively from the training partition (same scalar applied at inference)
  \item \textbf{WBC distribution}: Pneumonia cases show 15.4\% leukocytosis rate vs.\ 7.2\% in normal cases, confirming clinical discriminatory value of the biomarker
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Phase~1: Vision Backbone (Image-Only Baseline)}

The image encoder uses \textbf{DenseNet-121}~\cite{huang2017densenet} pre-trained on over 500{,}000 frontal chest radiographs via TorchXRayVision~\cite{cohen2022torchxrayvision}. Domain-specific pre-training is critical: pilot experiments replacing this backbone with ImageNet-pre-trained ResNet-50 caused AUC to drop from 0.826 to 0.713 on the scaled PA cohort, confirming the necessity of radiological domain transfer.

Images are normalised to the TorchXRayVision intensity range $[-1024, 1024]$, resized to $224 \times 224$, and preprocessed with contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalisation (\textbf{CLAHE}, clip limit 2.0, tile grid $8 \times 8$) to enhance subtle infiltrate contrast. Training augmentation includes random horizontal flip, rotation ($\pm10°$), small translation ($\pm5\%$), and Gaussian blur.

A \textbf{Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)}~\cite{woo2018cbam} is appended after the DenseNet backbone to direct the network toward diagnostically relevant pulmonary regions:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}' = \mathbf{M}_c(\mathbf{F}) \otimes \mathbf{F}
  \label{eq:cbam_channel}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{F}'' = \mathbf{M}_s(\mathbf{F}') \otimes \mathbf{F}'
  \label{eq:cbam_spatial}
\end{equation}
where the channel attention map is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{M}_c(\mathbf{F}) = \sigma\!\left(\text{MLP}\!\left(\text{AvgPool}(\mathbf{F})\right) + \text{MLP}\!\left(\text{MaxPool}(\mathbf{F})\right)\right)
  \label{eq:channel}
\end{equation}
with reduction ratio $r\!=\!16$, and the spatial attention map is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{M}_s(\mathbf{F}') = \sigma\!\left(f^{7\times7}\!\left([\text{AvgPool}_c(\mathbf{F}');\,\text{MaxPool}_c(\mathbf{F}')]\right)\right)
  \label{eq:spatial}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ denotes the sigmoid function and $f^{7\times7}$ is a $7\times7$ convolutional filter.

\textbf{Training configuration:} 5-fold GroupKFold cross-validation (grouped on \texttt{subject\_id}) with zero patient overlap, \textbf{Focal Loss}~\cite{lin2017focal} ($\gamma\!=\!2.0$, class weights from training distribution), AdamW optimiser ($\eta\!=\!10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), cosine annealing LR schedule, and 10-view \textbf{Test-Time Augmentation} (TTA) at inference. The encoder output is a \textbf{1024-dimensional} global average pooled feature vector $\mathbf{v}_{\text{img}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$, which is frozen for all downstream phases to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

\subsection{Phase~2: Radiology Text Fusion via Cross-Attention}

\textbf{Anti-leakage preprocessing} (detailed in Section~\ref{sec:antileak}) strips diagnostic IMPRESSION/CONCLUSION sections and redacts explicit pathology keywords from FINDINGS and HISTORY sections. The cleaned text is truncated/padded to 256 WordPiece tokens.

Text is encoded using \textbf{Bio\_ClinicalBERT}~\cite{alsentzer2019bert}, a 110M-parameter BERT-base model pre-trained on MIMIC-III clinical notes. The \textbf{top two transformer layers (10 and 11)} are unfrozen with a conservative learning rate $\eta_{\text{BERT}}\!=\!10^{-5}$ to enable task-specific adaptation while preventing catastrophic forgetting of clinical language representations; all lower layers remain frozen. The encoder emits the full token sequence $\mathbf{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 256 \times 768}$.

\textbf{8-head cross-attention fusion.} Image features act as queries and textual tokens as keys/values, allowing the model to identify which textual evidence is most relevant for the visual finding being analysed:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{c}_{\text{cross}} = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}_{\text{img}}\mathbf{K}_{\text{txt}}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf{V}_{\text{txt}}, \quad d_k = 64
  \label{eq:crossattn}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{Q}_{\text{img}} = \mathbf{v}_{\text{img}}\mathbf{W}_Q$ ($\mathbf{W}_Q \in \mathbb{R}^{1024 \times 512}$), and $\mathbf{K}_{\text{txt}}, \mathbf{V}_{\text{txt}}$ project each of the 256 text tokens through $\mathbf{W}_K, \mathbf{W}_V \in \mathbb{R}^{768 \times 512}$. After multi-head pooling and a linear projection, this yields $\mathbf{c}_{\text{cross}} \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$.

The classification head for Phase~2:
\begin{equation}
  [\mathbf{v}_{\text{img}} \,\|\, \mathbf{c}_{\text{cross}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{1536} \xrightarrow{\text{LN} \to 512 \to 128 \to 2} \hat{y}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Training configuration:} Focal Loss ($\gamma\!=\!2.0$), embedding Mixup~\cite{zhang2018mixup} ($\alpha\!=\!0.2$) applied in the fused embedding space, differential AdamW learning rates: fusion head at $2\!\times\!10^{-4}$, BERT layers at $10^{-5}$. Early stopping on validation AUC (patience 8). \textbf{Trainable parameters: $\approx$\,18M} (BERT layers 10--11: 14M; cross-attention: 3M; classifier: 1M).

\subsection{Phase~3c: WBC Triple Fusion (Main Conference Model)}

The WBC scalar is standardised using a \texttt{StandardScaler} fitted on the training split and projected through a dedicated three-layer non-linear encoder:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{e}_{\text{wbc}} = \text{MLP}(\text{wbc}): \; 1 \to 128 \to 128 \to 64
  \label{eq:wbcmlp}
\end{equation}
with ReLU activations and dropout $(p\!=\!0.3, p\!=\!0.2)$. This nonlinear projection is clinically motivated: a WBC of 12{,}000 vs.\ 16{,}000 vs.\ 28{,}000~cells/$\mu$L represents qualitatively distinct physiological states (borderline leukocytosis vs.\ acute infection vs.\ severe sepsis) that a linear transformation cannot adequately capture. The network can thus learn to respond to WBC in a threshold-like, non-proportional manner, mirroring clinical interpretation.

All three embeddings are concatenated to form the multimodal representation:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z} = [\,\mathbf{v}_{\text{img}} \,\|\, \mathbf{c}_{\text{cross}} \,\|\, \mathbf{e}_{\text{wbc}}\,] \in \mathbb{R}^{1600}
  \label{eq:concat}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}_{\text{img}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$, $\mathbf{c}_{\text{cross}} \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$, $\mathbf{e}_{\text{wbc}} \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$.

The classifier head processes this 1{,}600-dimensional vector:
\begin{equation}
  \text{LN}(1600) \to \text{Drop}(0.4) \to 512 \to \text{Drop}(0.3) \to 128 \to \text{Drop}(0.2) \to 2
  \label{eq:head}
\end{equation}
with GELU activations throughout.

\textbf{Warm-start protocol.} Phase~3c loads the trained Phase~2v2 checkpoint, preserving cross-attention alignment while initialising the WBC encoder from random weights. This warm-start reduces training time by $\approx$40\% compared to training from scratch. Differential learning rates: BERT\,=\,$10^{-5}$, fusion head\,=\,$2\!\times\!10^{-4}$, WBC encoder\,=\,$10^{-3}$. \textbf{Total trainable parameters: $\approx$\,18M.}

\textbf{Threshold calibration.} Decision thresholds are selected on the validation set only, using Youden's J statistic ($J = \text{TPR} - \text{FPR}$) for balanced operation and $\tau\!=\!0.500$ for emergency screening scenarios. The held-out test set is never used for threshold selection.

\subsection{Phase~4a: FiLM-Conditioned Dual Fusion (Real-World Deployment Variant)}
\label{sec:phase4a}

Many emergency departments---particularly in resource-limited settings---cannot reliably access digitised radiology reports within the first 15~minutes of patient arrival. Phase~4a addresses this with a \textbf{report-free} model that achieves strong performance using only the chest X-ray and the readily available WBC count.

Rather than concatenating a WBC embedding with image features, Phase~4a uses \textbf{Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)}~\cite{perez2018film} to allow the WBC scalar to dynamically rescale and shift the frozen DenseNet-121 feature channels:
\begin{equation}
  \text{FiLM}(\mathbf{v}_{\text{img}}; \gamma, \beta) = \gamma \odot \mathbf{v}_{\text{img}} + \beta
  \label{eq:film}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma, \beta \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$ are predicted by a lightweight \textbf{FiLM generator}:
\begin{equation}
  [\gamma,\, \beta] = \text{MLP}_{\text{film}}(\text{wbc}): \; 1 \to 256 \to 2048
  \label{eq:filmgen}
\end{equation}
The generator outputs a 2048-dimensional vector that is split into two 1024-dimensional vectors $\gamma$ and $\beta$. This formulation allows the WBC value to amplify feature channels associated with inflammatory consolidation (high $\gamma_i$ when WBC is elevated) and suppress channels linked to benign opacity patterns.

The modulated features pass through a classification head:
\begin{equation}
  \text{FiLM}(\mathbf{v}_{\text{img}}) \xrightarrow{\text{LN} \to \text{Drop}(0.3) \to 512 \to 256 \to 2} \hat{y}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Training configuration:} The DenseNet-121 ImageEncoder is fully frozen (all parameters locked). Only the FiLM generator and classifier head are trained, yielding \textbf{$<$1M trainable parameters} (vs.\ $\sim$18M for Phase~3c). Focal Loss ($\gamma\!=\!2.0$) and dual Mixup (applied jointly to image features and WBC scalar). AdamW, $\eta\!=\!5\!\times\!10^{-4}$, early stopping (patience 10). This ultra-lightweight design makes Phase~4a suitable for edge deployment on low-power clinical hardware.

\textbf{Interpretability.} The FiLM $\gamma$ and $\beta$ vectors provide direct auditability: for any patient, one can inspect which of the 1024 feature channels were amplified ($\gamma_i \gg 1$) or suppressed ($\gamma_i \ll 1$) in response to their WBC value, offering mechanistic insight absent in standard concatenation models.

% ==========================================================
%  V. ANTI-LEAKAGE PROTOCOL
% ==========================================================
\section{\textcolor{aideep}{Anti-Leakage Protocol}}
\label{sec:antileak}

A critical concern in multimodal medical AI is diagnostic label leakage through text. Radiology reports contain an IMPRESSION section that directly states the radiologist's diagnosis. Models trained on raw reports achieve artificially high performance by memorising this conclusion rather than learning from clinical evidence. To our knowledge, this risk is not explicitly mitigated in most prior multimodal CXR classification systems~\cite{kovaleva2020towards,moon2022multimodal}.

PneumoFusionNet enforces the following multi-layer safeguards:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Impression Removal:} IMPRESSION and CONCLUSION sections are stripped via regular-expression parsing before tokenisation. Only FINDINGS and HISTORY sections are retained.
  \item \textbf{Keyword Redaction:} Diagnostic trigger words (e.g., ``pneumonia'', ``pneumonic'', ``lobar consolidation'', ``compatible with infection'', ``no acute cardiopulmonary process'') in remaining text are replaced with \texttt{[REDACTED]}.
  \item \textbf{Patient-Level Splitting:} All partitions enforce strict grouping on \texttt{subject\_id} using GroupKFold (Phase~1) and GroupShuffleSplit (Phases~2--4a). Automated verification confirms: Train $\cap$ Val\,=\,$\emptyset$, Train $\cap$ Test\,=\,$\emptyset$, Val $\cap$ Test\,=\,$\emptyset$.
  \item \textbf{PA-Only Filtering:} AP views were removed after pilot experiments revealed that view type correlated with patient acuity (ICU patients receive bedside AP scans), creating an acquisition-bias shortcut.
  \item \textbf{Train-Only Imputation:} Missing WBC values (15\%) are imputed using the training-set median. The same scalar is applied to validation and test sets without leaking test distribution information.
  \item \textbf{Leakage Sanity Check:} A model trained on \emph{only} the redacted IMPRESSION text (after stripping) achieved AUC\,=\,0.512, confirming no residual diagnostic signal in the stripped text.
\end{enumerate}

Table~\ref{tab:results} reveals a clear progressive trajectory. The image-only Phase~1 model exhibits a hard ceiling at AUC\,=\,0.826 despite extensive optimisation (CBAM, Focal Loss, TTA, 5-fold CV), confirming that visual features alone are insufficient for reliable pneumonia detection. Phase~4a---using CXR and WBC without text---provides a substantial AUC gain to 0.895 with only $<$1M trainable parameters and no radiology report requirement, demonstrating the value of even a single laboratory biomarker as a low-cost complement to imaging. Adding structured radiology text (Phase~2v2) achieves AUC\,=\,0.946. The full triple fusion (Phase~3c) reaches AUC\,=\,0.971, outperforming even the 17-feature full EHR panel (AUC\,=\,0.969) with notably higher sensitivity (92.3\% vs.\ 89.1\%), while requiring only the WBC value as a laboratory input.

% ==========================================================
%  VII. DISCUSSION
% ==========================================================
\section{\textcolor{aideep}{Discussion}}

\textbf{Multimodal learning substantially outperforms image-only approaches.} The image-only model plateaued at AUC\,=\,0.826 despite extensive architectural optimisation. This ceiling reflects a fundamental limitation: chest radiographs encode morphological structure but not physiological state---the duration and severity of systemic inflammation, host immune response, or pre-existing comorbidities. Adding clinical context breaks this ceiling decisively.

\textbf{FiLM conditioning enables effective scalar-to-image modulation.} Phase~4a demonstrates that a WBC scalar can meaningfully modulate frozen deep features through FiLM, achieving +6.9~pp AUC gain over the image baseline with no radiology text and $<$1M trainable parameters. The learned $\gamma$ values reveal that channels sensitive to bilateral infiltrate patterns are consistently upweighted when WBC is elevated, providing clinically interpretable feature attribution without Grad-CAM.

\textbf{Cross-attention enables selective image-text alignment.} The 8-head cross-attention mechanism allows image features to selectively attend to relevant textual tokens. When the radiograph shows an ambiguous opacity, the model attends to descriptive terms such as ``consolidation'', ``fever'', or ``productive cough'' in the FINDINGS section, disambiguating pathology from benign atelectasis or effusion.

\textbf{Strict anti-leakage is essential for valid evaluation.} Removing the IMPRESSION section and redacting diagnostic keywords ensures that observed performance reflects genuine multimodal learning. Our sanity check---training on stripped IMPRESSION text alone achieving AUC\,=\,0.512---confirms the protocol's effectiveness. This stands in contrast to systems that train on raw reports and likely overestimate real-world performance.

\textbf{A single biomarker provides substantial diagnostic value.} Although WBC is only one scalar measurement, its integration over image-text fusion contributed +2.5~pp AUC, +5.6~pp sensitivity, and +4.5~pp specificity. A CBC result is universally available, inexpensive, and obtained within 15~minutes of admission, making the CXR\,+\,Report\,+\,WBC triad feasible within 30~minutes. By contrast, the 17-feature full EHR panel (Phase~3 Full) delivered slightly lower sensitivity (89.1\%) and required 1--4~hours of laboratory processing, suggesting that targeted single-biomarker fusion is preferable to comprehensive but delayed panel integration.

\textbf{Phase~4a as a deployable fallback.} In scenarios where radiology reports are unavailable (resource-limited hospitals, point-of-care devices, telemedicine), Phase~4a provides a viable alternative achieving AUC\,=\,0.895 and 84.5\% sensitivity---substantially better than any image-only system and requiring only a standard CBC. The model fits on a CPU and could be embedded in a tablet-based clinical decision support tool.

\textbf{Limitations.} All data originate from a single US institution (BIDMC). The binary classification formulation does not address multi-label co-morbidities. Approximately 15\% of WBC values required median imputation. External validation on diverse populations, scanner hardware, and clinical workflows (e.g., Indian subcontinent hospitals) is essential before clinical deployment.

% ==========================================================
%  VIII. CONCLUSION
% ==========================================================
\section{\textcolor{aideep}{Conclusion}}

We presented PneumoFusionNet, a leakage-controlled progressive multimodal framework that integrates chest radiographs, anti-leakage radiology report text, and White Blood Cell count for pneumonia detection on 3{,}763 MIMIC-CXR studies. The proposed approach achieved systematic improvements from image-only learning (AUC\,=\,0.826) through image-text cross-attention fusion (AUC\,=\,0.946) to image-text-WBC triple fusion (AUC\,=\,0.971, Sensitivity\,=\,92.3\%, Specificity\,=\,93.6\%), outperforming published SOTA systems by 5.0~pp AUC while using fewer clinical features. The lightweight FiLM-conditioned Phase~4a variant (AUC\,=\,0.895, $<$1M params, no text required) provides a practical deployment pathway for resource-limited or time-critical settings. Both the triple-fusion and dual-fusion models operate within a 15--30~minute emergency admission window. Future work will investigate external validation on geographically diverse hospital datasets, multi-label pathology classification, and additional laboratory biomarkers (CRP, procalcitonin) as complementary inflammatory markers.
